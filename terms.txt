Sociology | The scientific study of human society, social behavior, and social relationships
Sociological imagination | The ability to see how personal experiences are connected to larger social forces and history
Culture | The shared beliefs, values, norms, language, and material objects of a group of people
Society | A group of people who live in a defined territory and share a culture
Socialization | The lifelong process of learning the norms, values, and behaviors of one's society
Norms | Shared rules and expectations about how people should behave in particular situations
Values | Shared beliefs about what is good, right, and desirable in a society
Folkways | Informal norms for everyday behavior that carry mild consequences when broken
Mores | Strongly held norms that carry moral weight and serious consequences when violated
Deviance | Behavior that violates the norms of a group or society
Social structure | The patterned social arrangements in society that shape how people act
Social stratification | The ranking of people into layers based on wealth, power, and prestige
Social class | A group of people who share a similar economic position and lifestyle
Status | A position a person holds in society, such as student, parent, or friend
Role | The behaviors and obligations expected of a person who holds a particular status
Social control | The methods a society uses to encourage conformity and discourage deviance
Stereotype | An oversimplified and generalized belief about a group of people
Prejudice | A negative attitude toward a group of people based on preconceived ideas
Discrimination | Unequal treatment of people based on their group membership
Functionalism | A theory that views society as a system of interdependent parts that work together for stability
